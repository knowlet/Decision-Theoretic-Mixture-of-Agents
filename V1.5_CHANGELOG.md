# v1.5 evidence and implementation changes

## Locked specification

`transfer_protocol.json` SHA-256 `40e677546278ad27fa7d58c6437afe969a5054d3f8d5d0fc4cab1d3c4d23165c` and `openjev_protocol.json` SHA-256 `b377a1d8f446b416e0507a8e4181a2ec0a28bbf0411a52dde0a367a43e460240` are committed together with the code that reads them, before the v1.5 policy outcomes and before the widened selector pilot was scored. This is a prospective project checkpoint, not independent preregistration. Objective, costs, pools, contrasts and policies are unchanged from v1.3.

## What changed

- Two datasets are added from the same immutable ProEval revision: `gqa` (visual question answering, 2,000 questions) and `jigsaw` (toxicity verdicts, 1,500 questions). Both were checked to contain all eight fixed worker identities of both pools. Primary binary tasks go from four to six; pinned files go from five to seven once the DICES ordinal diagnostic is counted.
- The split salt string `dtmoa-proeval-v13` is retained verbatim. The four previously published datasets therefore keep their exact train/development/test assignment, and their per-task results are expected to reproduce the earlier release exactly. That expectation is checked against the published v1.3.0 table rather than assumed.
- Answer identities for the added tasks are implemented and documented in the protocol: a VQA normalization subset for the free-form `gqa` answers (NFKC casefold, punctuation removed, articles dropped, number words zero-to-ten folded to digits), and a fold of the common spellings of the verbal `jigsaw` verdicts.
- The derived `jigsaw` verdict is "toxic iff the annotator toxic-fraction is strictly greater than 0.5". That rule was validated against the archive's own binary label column and reproduces it on all 11,998 comparable pairs. The archive's own label column remains the primary error either way.
- The selector pilot is widened to all six primary datasets: 192 test and 96 development groups instead of 128 and 64, with the group-selection salt unchanged so every previously scored case is still selected. Shards scale from eight to twelve (4B) and two to three (0.8B) to hold the per-runner forward budget.
- Every literal case count in the shard and analysis gates is now derived from the locked protocols, and the manuscript counts are filled from the audited artifacts, so widening the task pool cannot leave a stale number or gate behind.

## Disclosures added by this version

- The pinned public `gqa` release contains nine prediction cells holding a data-generation debug string instead of a model answer. Six of them fall on the eight model identities replayed here (four legacy, two replacement); those are treated as unparseable answers and counted in the source audit, and the archive's own label for them is not silently rescored. This is a defect of the upstream artifact, not a property of the models.
- The `gqa` archive was produced by models that could see the referenced image. The replay stores and reads only the question text and image id, so only answer selection is replayed; the text-only prompt controls are handicapped on that task and the paper says so.
- Under the declared loss, `gqa` is deferral-dominated: the best archived candidate error exceeds the 0.25 deferral loss, so the decision-theoretic controls withhold on every held-out case and the task carries no policy discrimination. It is retained in the primary average and reported rather than excluded after the outcome was seen. `policy_discrimination.csv` records the per-task, per-phase spread and deferral rate.
- A strict-gold audit separates literal-normalized answers from the archive's own labels. On `gqa` they disagree on 177 of 16,000 comparable pairs (2.0% of replacement-pool pairs), mostly where the archive counts article-only differences as errors. Both numbers are reported; neither is presented as ground truth about model reasoning.

## Scope

The actual experiment uses historical ProEval outputs, not sklearn classifier proxies and not live workers. The cumulative-score ridge/TF-IDF control is neither CERA-MoA nor Jev. DICES remains a separate complete-case diagnostic excluded from binary headline comparisons. Pool replacement does not represent continuous training checkpoints. Model-training steps, live LLM calls, proprietary Jev calls and new worker generation remain zero; the only new neural inference is local OpenJev selector scoring over archived answers.

The added tasks are widely used public benchmarks, so familiarity and contamination are not excluded. Six datasets from one archive are not six independent data-collection pipelines, and they still do not represent open-ended debate, tool use or value disagreement. Older versions and their papers remain unchanged.
